## 3. What is actually affordable, and what it costs in credibility

| r | total h | statistical standing |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 9.1 | **Too few.** Elementary effects are means over r samples; r = 2 gives a standard error on μ* that cannot separate factors of similar influence. Reports a ranking it cannot defend. |
| 4 | 18.1 | **Minimum defensible.** Coarse but honest for *identifying the clearly-dominant and clearly-negligible* factors, which is all a screening step is asked for. |
| 10 | 45.3 | The literature default; **out of budget by 5x.** |

## 4. Recommendation — do NOT simply run r = 2 to fit the number

The temptation is to pick whichever r lands near 8.8 h and call it Morris. That would produce a factor ranking with no defensible standard error, reported as if it had one — the same class of error as a manufactured null (`DECISIONS.md` D6) or a timeout reported as non-convergence (`CLAUDE.md` §4.15). Three legitimate routes instead:

1. **Screen the tower-class factors separately and for free.** SM and t_TES need no rebuild, so a full-resolution Morris over just those two costs minutes against a single cached map. Do this first — it is nearly free and answers two of the eight factors properly.
2. **Cut the map cost, not the trajectory count.** The 38-min build is at the sweep resolution used for the envelope. A coarser map (fewer points per axis) is cheaper, and n=3 vs n=5 moved annual energy by only **+0.428 %** — a screening step cares about factor *ranking*, which that shift is very unlikely to reorder. Establish the cheapest map whose ranking matches, then run r = 10 on it.
3. **If neither, run r = 4 (18.1 h) and report it as coarse**, with the standard error on μ* printed beside every factor so the reader can see which separations the design actually supports.

⚠ **Whichever is chosen, record it in `DECISIONS.md`** — the same rule D6 was held to. A screening design chosen by budget rather than by statistics is a defensible decision only if it is a *stated* one.
